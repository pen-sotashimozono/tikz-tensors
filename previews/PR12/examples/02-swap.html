Exchanging two fermions

Exchanging two fermions flips the sign of the amplitude.

Straight legs, so these are finite indices: two modes of a basis, i and j, rather than two positions. Psi is then an array of numbers – coefwide, not a function of position – and the statement is the antisymmetry of that array. The wavy form of the same picture, where the arguments are continuous, is the expansion in 01; tests/cases/styles.tex draws \tnswap both ways.

Both sides are the same stack: Psi across two sites, and under it a layer of ‘|’ that the indices run through. The crossing needs that room, and the straight side has it too, so the indices of both end at one depth.

The picture

\begin{tikzpicture}
  \tnstack{P}{2}{state, legs}
  \tnlayer{P}{state}{coefwide/$\Psi$/2}
  \tnlayer{P}{legs}{2*|}
  \tnopenswap[edge=disc, labels={$i$, $j$}]{P-state-1}
  \tneq[factor=$-$]
  \tnstack{Q}{2}{state, legs}
  \tnlayer{Q}{state}{coefwide/$\Psi$/2}
  \tnlayer{Q}{legs}{2*|}
  \tnopen[labels={$i$, $j$}]{down}{Q}
\end{tikzpicture}
