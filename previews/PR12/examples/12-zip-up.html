Zip-up

Zip-up: an MPO applied to an MPS and compressed in one sweep, caught in the middle of it.

The MPS comes in right-canonical, B. Left of the zipper, the sweep is done: each step contracted one W into one B, split the result by a truncated SVD, and left the left factor behind – the new MPS, isometric, A tilde. The zipper C carries the rest of what has been contracted: one index to the left into the new MPS, one into each layer on the right, where B and W have not been touched yet, and the physical index of its site. Next step it absorbs the B and W of site 4 and splits again.

C is one entry across both layers of its site, so it is as tall as they are and has an index into each, which is what makes it the seam between the two halves. It is drawn as a plain array because that is what it is: neither half of the gauge.

The picture

\begin{tikzpicture}
  \tnstack{Z}{5}{ket, op}
  \tnlayer{Z}{ket}{2*., coef/$C$/1/2, 2*canr/$B$}
  \tnlayer{Z}{op}{2*canl/$\tilde A$, ., 2*mpo/$W$}
  \tnconnect{Z}
  \tnopen[label=$\sigma_{#1}$]{down}{Z}
\end{tikzpicture}
