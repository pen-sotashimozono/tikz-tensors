Mixed canonical form

A finite matrix product state in mixed canonical form, with a two-site gate about to be applied: one step of TEBD. The triangles say which tensors are isometric – canl to the left of the center, canr to the right – and the arrows on the bonds say which way the gauge runs. The center is the one tensor that is not isometric, and it is also the one drawn as a diamond: shape and colour both single it out, which is the point, because the norm and every local expectation value can be read off it alone.

A stack of two layers, the state and the gate. The arrows are not written: a bond takes its direction from the triangles it joins, away from one pointing right and into one pointing left, so every arrow runs toward the center wherever the center is put. The physical index leaves each triangle at the corner of its flat side and continues that side's line.

The gate has no legs of its own. The indices run past it and a node covers an edge, so it interrupts them – which is what applying a gate to those indices looks like. The sites it does not touch are ‘|’ in its layer, so all five indices run on to one depth.

Finite, so the chain simply stops: the outer indices are open, with nothing at the far end, and point inward with the gauge.
